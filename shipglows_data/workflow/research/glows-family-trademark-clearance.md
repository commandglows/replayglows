# Research: Glows family trademark clearance

> Updated 2026-08-05 — 52 sources — France/EU focus with international collision screening

## Executive Summary

None of the four candidates can responsibly be described as cleared. No exact indexed hit was found for the plural compounds `DeskGlows`, `WorkGlows`, or `PilotGlows`, but each collides with a very close singular or reversed-order sign already used in software or digital services. `Glows` alone is the weakest option because the `GLOW/GLOWS` field is crowded in software and education. The safest conclusion is to keep searching for a more distinctive `[qualifier]Glows` compound rather than file one of these four immediately.

This is a documented preliminary clearance, not a legal opinion. INPI itself describes a name search as only a first check and recommends similarity research; its database covers French, EU, and international marks relevant to France. [INPI](https://www.inpi.fr/ressources/propriete-intellectuelle/rechercher-une-marque-base-marques)

## Scope and method

The screening covered exact spellings, spacing, singular/plural forms, reversed word order, `Glow/Glows/Glowz` variants, active commercial uses, and goods/services relevant to Nice classes 9, 35, 41, and 42. WIPO warns that its global database does not replace searches of national and regional registers. [WIPO](https://www.wipo.int/en/web/global-brand-database)

The legal comparison is not limited to exact identity: EUIPO assesses visual, phonetic, and conceptual similarity, the relatedness of goods/services, the relevant public, and the distinctiveness of the earlier mark. [EUIPO](https://guidelines.euipo.europa.eu/2302857/2227748/trade-mark-guidelines/4-2-list-of-factors-for-assessing-the-likelihood-of-confusion)

## Candidate findings

### DeskGlows — high commercial collision risk; medium-to-high legal screening risk

- No exact indexed record for `DESKGLOWS` was located in the searches performed.
- `GlowDesk` is already actively used by several software services, including a German booking, cash-register, staff-management, analytics, and AI platform. This is an EU commercial use in a closely related digital-services field. [GlowDesk Germany](https://www.glowdesk.de/)
- `GlowDesk Inc.` operates an AI receptionist platform and expressly claims rights in its software and branding. [GlowDesk terms](https://glowdeskapp.com/app/terms-of-service)
- An active Canadian corporation named `Deskglow Inc.` has existed since 2023; the official Canadian trademark record for its registered `KOLU` mark confirms the owner identity. [CIPO](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/cipo/trademark-search/pdf/2335471?lang=eng)

Assessment: pluralising `DeskGlow` does little to distinguish it, while reversing `GlowDesk` to `DeskGlows` preserves both dominant verbal elements and a closely related concept. This does not prove infringement, but it makes the name unsuitable for a low-risk launch without a professional register search and owner-by-owner analysis.

### WorkGlows — high risk

- No exact indexed record for `WORKGLOWS` was located, but `WORKGLOW` is an extremely close earlier sign.
- `WORKGLOW` was registered in the United States for web-based workforce-management and HR software in class 42; that registration was cancelled in 2017, so it is not itself an active US registration, but it establishes substantial earlier use in precisely the software/productivity field. [Justia record](https://trademarks.justia.com/778/64/workglow-77864486.html)
- `workglow.app` currently markets a desktop application for local AI workflows on macOS, Windows, and Linux. [workglow.app](https://workglow.app/)
- `workglow.dev` currently markets a browser-based AI workflow builder and identifies related desktop and enterprise products. [workglow.dev](https://workglow.dev/)
- A UK word mark `WORKGLOW`, application UK00003910593, is registered through 2033 in class 9 for high-visibility protective clothing. Its goods are remote from productivity consulting, but the live registration confirms that the word is already appropriated in a nearby territory. [UK record mirror](https://www.trademarkelite.com/uk/trademark/trademark-detail/UK00003910593/Workglow)

Assessment: the final `s` is unlikely to create strong separation from `WORKGLOW`, and the active desktop/AI workflow product overlaps strongly with Windows productivity. This is the weakest compound of the three.

### PilotGlows — medium-to-high risk

- No exact indexed record for `PILOTGLOWS` or `PILOTGLOW` was located in the searches performed.
- `GlowPilot` is already actively used for an AI-powered clinic-management platform covering scheduling, analytics, recommendations, training, and software services. [GlowPilot](https://getglowpilot.com/)
- The service's current terms identify `GlowPilot` as the operator-facing name of the software platform. [GlowPilot terms](https://getglowpilot.com/terms)

Assessment: `PilotGlows` reverses the same two components and adds a final `s`. The sectors are not identical, but both signs concern AI-enabled business software and operational improvement. The inverted construction reduces similarity somewhat, but not enough to call the name clear.

### Glows — very high risk / poor registrability strategy

- `GLOWS` has appeared in multiple trademark records. An earlier US registration covered accounting software in class 9 and was cancelled in 2016. [Historical record](https://furm.com/trademarks/glows-73367975)
- A current US application for `GLOWS` concerns tomatoes in class 31; the goods are unrelated, but it shows continued appropriation of the exact word. [USPTO record mirror](https://www.trademarkelite.com/trademark/trademark-detail/98681885/GLOWS)
- `Glows.ai` actively supplies software and states that its product and documentation are protected by trademark and other intellectual-property laws in the US, EU, and elsewhere. [Glows.ai EULA](https://glows.ai/eula)
- Numerous active `GLOW` software marks exist internationally, including Amazon's education/software offering and Glow App Limited's software and education services. [Amazon GLOW record](https://uspto.report/TM/97337308) [Glow App record](https://trademarks.justia.com/904/82/glow-90482663.html)

Assessment: even if an exact French/EU `GLOWS` registration were absent for the final specification, the field is crowded and enforcement would be difficult. `Glows` works better as a family convention than as the sole distinctive element of a new mark.

## Recommended filing perimeter

For a Windows-productivity advice business, the final goods/services list should be drafted narrowly around the actual offer, with at least:

- class 35 for business organisation and productivity consultancy when genuinely offered;
- class 41 for training, courses, tutorials, and educational publication;
- class 42 only for IT consultancy, SaaS, or software services actually intended;
- class 9 only for downloadable software, applications, or downloadable educational products actually intended.

Class numbers alone do not decide similarity; EUIPO compares the nature, purpose, users, method of use, competition, complementarity, channels, public, and usual commercial origin of the goods/services. [EUIPO goods/services guidance](https://guidelines.euipo.europa.eu/1803468/1786759/trade-mark-guidelines/3-1-1-similarity-factors)

## Recommendation

Do not invest in domains, logos, incorporation changes, or an application for any of these four names yet. Generate a new shortlist that retains the family suffix `Glows` but uses a more distinctive first element, then run exact and similarity searches before public use. If `PilotGlows` must remain under consideration, obtain an INPI similarity search and a French/EU trademark attorney opinion covering classes 35, 41, and any genuinely needed parts of 9 and 42.

### Deeper KeyGlow prior-right assessment

- No current exact `KEYGLOW`, `KEY GLOW`, or `KEYGLOWS` registration was independently confirmed from an accessible official-register result in this investigation. WIPO, EUIPO/TMview, and DPMA interfaces must still be searched manually or through counsel before any legal conclusion about registrations.
- Absence of a confirmed registration would not make the name low-risk. German law separately protects business designations and work titles used in trade under section 5 MarkenG. [German Federal Ministry of Justice — MarkenG §5](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/markeng/__5.html)
- EUIPO guidance also recognises certain non-registered marks and other signs used in trade as possible earlier rights, subject to proof, national law, and use of more than merely local significance. [EUIPO — Article 8(4) earlier signs](https://guidelines.euipo.europa.eu/2302857/2045383/trade-mark-guidelines)
- Digital Design UG is identified on the official store listing at a German address, and public company-register-derived information links the company and its director to online marketing, web design, affiliate marketing, and IT-company participation. [Digital Design UG company record](https://www.webvalid.de/company/Digital%2BDesign%2BUG%2B%28haftungsbeschr%C3%A4nkt%29%2C%2BUelzen/HRB%2B209759)
- The indexed KeyGlow terms expressly claim the KeyGlow name and logo as trademarks. This wording is not evidence of registration, but it is evidence that the operator has asserted proprietary rights rather than disclaimed the name. [KeyGlow terms](https://www.keyglow.app/terms-and-conditions)

Practical conclusion: because `KeyGlows` differs only by a final plural `s`, targets the same broad digital/browser context, and would be launched globally, it is not worth paying to negotiate or litigate around this antecedent when the naming brief explicitly prioritises minimum risk. A consent or coexistence agreement could theoretically reduce conflict, but it would add cost, dependency, territorial limits, and enforcement uncertainty to a brand that has not yet launched.

The corresponding `.com` strings returned no domain record from Verisign RDAP at the time checked: `keyglows.com`, `browsinglows.com`, `computinglows.com`, `thinkinglows.com`, `clickinglows.com`, and `upgradinglows.com`. A missing RDAP record is only a point-in-time registration indicator, not a reservation, ownership right, registrar guarantee, or trademark clearance. [Verisign RDAP](https://rdap.verisign.com/com/v1/)

### Linguistic warning

The shared-`g` construction is legible in title case (`BrowsinGlows`) but loses that boundary in case-insensitive domains and handles (`browsinglows`). English readers can parse the latter as “browsing lows”; the same problem affects `computinglows`, `thinkinglows`, `clickinglows`, and `upgradinglows`. This is a brand-comprehension risk independent of trademark availability.

### What the existing TubeFlow products do—and do not—prove

The active `tubeflow.ai` competitor openly describes itself as an AI video-research workspace for YouTube and identifies Coffee Flow Labs in its terms. Its existence proves that a founder can currently operate a YouTube-centered service containing `Tube` in its name; it does not prove Google approval, trademark registration, clearance, or immunity from a later complaint.

The name is already collision-prone. A separate `tubeflow.co` service uses the same `TubeFlow` name for YouTube Shorts creation, upload, playlist management, and API permissions under North Carolina terms. A separate mobile application is listed as `tubeflow1` for organizing YouTube subscriptions. These concurrent uses suggest weak exclusivity and user-confusion risk rather than a clean precedent to copy.

Google's published branding rule expressly names `YouTube`, `YT`, `You-Tube`, and derivatives, but does not state that every compound containing the ordinary word `Tube` is automatically prohibited. Therefore `TubeFlow` may be tolerated, may fall outside Google's interpretation of “derivative,” may not yet have been reviewed, or may simply be operating under accepted residual risk. No public evidence located establishes which explanation applies.

## Confidence and limitations

Confidence is medium. Official guidance and identified records/usages are reliable, but live register interfaces impose technical access restrictions, and indexed-web absence is not proof of register absence. The report does not determine ownership priority, territorial reputation, genuine use, validity, or the precise scope of every identified registration. Those questions require live register extracts and legal analysis of the final specification.
